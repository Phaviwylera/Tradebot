Opus Agent Desk

Six agents. One decision. Paper only.

Ready

Scan the board. Let risk say no.

The desk pulls the most active Polymarket contracts, finds unusual moves, checks dated headlines and price structure, writes one rule-based plan per candidate, and refuses anything that breaks a limit. There is no order button, and never will be.

1 · Scoutunusual price, volume and movement
2 · Newsdated sources, facts vs speculation
3 · Chartstrend, momentum, support, resistance
4 · Strategyentry, invalidation, target, time limit
5 · Riskveto power over every plan
6 · Bossapprove, wait or reject

What this is not

  • No orders. No wallet, key, signer or execution endpoint exists in this project.
  • No model guessing. All six agents are deterministic code. There is no language model in the loop.
  • No promised edge. A clean report means the workflow completed safely, not that the trade wins.
  • No hidden fees. Risk charges spread and a configurable cost buffer before it approves anything.
  • No averaging. Disagreements go to the Boss as WAIT, not into a blended score.
Where this kind of setup usually fails
  • Unusual ≠ tradable. A volume spike is attention, not an edge. Most spikes are already priced in.
  • Headlines are claims. A keyword feed cannot verify a report or read the body of an article. Missing sources must stop a plan, not be filled in.
  • Levels on thin probability books are noisy. Support and resistance are treated as context, never as a guarantee.
  • Fees decide marginal trades. The risk gate charges spread and a cost buffer before it approves; many 1.5:1 setups die there.
  • Synthetic trends are kind. Test-mode approval rates say nothing about live performance. Paper results do not prove an edge either.
  • Six agents are not independent. They share inputs and one codebase. Treat the pipeline as a checklist, not a committee of experts.

Paper-trading research, not financial advice. The full review and method notes are in docs/REVIEW.md and docs/HOW_IT_WORKS.md in the repository.
